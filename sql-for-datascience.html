Data Quality Analysis for Hashed Email to MAID Linkage

Background

Several data buyers reached out regarding inaccuracies and overmatching of our data. The dataset they were inquiring about was Hashed Email to Mobile Advertising ID (MAID) linkage and in this case, overmatching meant that the same MAID was being linked to 10 to 12 different MAIDs. Since a MAID is supposed to represent a single device and be deterministic, this would indicate that on average the same email address was being linked to 10 to 12 different MAIDs. This was a problem because while running ad campaigns, such high matches lead to low performance and lost the partner revenue. This led them to question the accuracy and value of our data

To create this dataset, we were ingesting linkage data from partners, enriching it with signals from our ad exchange and transforming it into into a new product used for targeted marketing and advertising. Data was being ingested from more than 50 partners and there was no alerting regarding the quality of the data being ingested, so finding the dirty data would be a challenge.

Approach

While there was no active reporting, all of the data lived in a Snowflake data warehouse and could be queried using SQL. Using queries, I was able to create a report that showed 1) Total number of Hashed Email to MAID linkages, 2) Number of unique, distinct pairs delivered, 3) Percentage overlap of linkages that matched with other partners, and 4) A breakdown of revenue earned by each partner/buyer intersection.

Results

By running this report, I was able to identify that the majority of the overmatching was being caused by 10% of data providers and was able to temporarily removing these providers from the dataset while I worked with them to tweak the parameters they used to provide us data. Removing these providers from the dataset led to an immediate, significant increase in performance of ad campaigns from the partner that was purchasing data from us and an increase in revenue for our company. After working with our suppliers and resolving the issues, we were able to reintegrate the data and performance remained high.

Most importantly, this project led to the creation of active reporting that could be used to flag and omit data not compliant with our quality standards and allowed us to maintain the integrity of our data and the performance of our products. I led scoping efforts with product and engineering to create an automated version of the reports I put together during this project.
